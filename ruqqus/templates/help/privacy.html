Videos from other sites

Loading a video site's player lets that site set cookies, see your address and record what you watch. So {{ 'SITE_NAME' | app_config }} asks first in the UK, the EEA and Switzerland, and wherever it can't tell which country you are in. It tells from the country the network in front of the site adds to each request, if there is one, and doesn't keep it for this.

  • Until you answer, and after Not now, no video site's player loads until you press play, and a press loads that one player. Its thumbnail can still come from that site as you reach it. That covers feeds and post pages, a video replay on a Space's page and a YouTube video in a story.
  • After Allow videos, players load by themselves, muted, as they scroll into view on a feed or a post page, as they do elsewhere. A video replay on a Space's page then loads as it comes into view, and a YouTube video in a story when that story comes up.

Your answer is kept only in your browser, in the video_consent cookie, and we keep no record of it. If you make an account where we ask, the sign-up form asks instead of the banner. How often pages are asked for

{{ 'SITE_NAME' | app_config }} counts how many requests its pages get, on our own server and with no outside service. For each day (in UTC) it adds one to three totals: one for the kind of page ("a post", "a profile"), one for the site that linked to it (its name only, never the address of the page you were on, and "this site" or "none" when that is what it was), and one for the country, where the network in front of the site tells us. These are requests, not people: the totals can't say how many people there were, and nothing ties them to you or to each other. No account, name, IP address, browser or page address is kept with them, and nothing is stored in your browser for them. We read the name of your browser only to leave out what says it is a robot. Pages a script fetches for itself and pages in a side panel aren't counted. The totals are kept for 400 days and only site admins see them.

Ads

{{ 'SITE_NAME' | app_config }} shows ads from Google AdSense on feeds and post pages and in the right sidebar beside them. Premium members see no ads, and neither does anyone on the Child word filter, which includes every child account. Settings, chat, sign-in, search and help pages have none, and neither do a shared draft's pages.

We check what is written on a page before ads go on it. A whole page goes without ads if a post on it is private, removed, marked sensitive, rated by the word filter, by a sensitive account or in a private, Circle or sensitive guild, and a post on this site that another post quotes counts the same way when it is shown. So does a page with a comment that is marked sensitive, rated or by a sensitive account, or that holds a picture or a sound; a post page with neither text nor comments; and a post page with a live thread that is still live, or one that has ended with a rated update or summary, or a picture or sound in one. An anonymous post or comment is never judged by who wrote it.

That is all we check. Comments that load later, when you press to see more, are not checked, and neither is what a post shows from another site (a link preview, a post quoted from elsewhere, a video), a post's own picture, a profile's bio or the other boxes in the sidebar.

Google and its partners use cookies and similar technology to show ads and to measure them. In the UK, the EEA and Switzerland they ask for your consent first, through Google's consent banner. You can change your choice at any time with Privacy and cookie settings (also in the page footer). If you see no ads, Google's script is not loaded on this page until you press that link.

We ask Google for non-personalised ads in the UK, the EEA and Switzerland, wherever we can't tell which country you are in, whenever you are not signed in, and for any account that hasn't told us it is 18 or over. Only an account that told us it is 18 or over, outside those places, can get personalised ads, and you can turn those off in Google's ad settings. Non-personalised ads still use cookies, for example to limit how often an ad is shown and to stop fraud.
